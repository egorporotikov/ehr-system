What is an EHR System?

Understanding Electronic Health Records

Overview

An Electronic Health Record (EHR) is a digital version of a patient's paper chart. EHRs are real-time, patient-centered records that make information available instantly and securely to authorized users.

Unlike traditional paper records, EHRs contain the medical and treatment histories of patients and are designed to go beyond standard clinical data collected in a provider's office to include a broader view of a patient's care.

What EHRs Include

  • Patient demographics: Name, age, gender
  • Medical history: Past conditions and treatments
  • Medications: Current and past prescriptions
  • Allergies: Known drug and food allergies
  • Immunization status: Vaccination records
  • Laboratory test results: Blood work, cultures
  • Radiology images: X-rays, MRIs, CT scans
  • Vital signs: Blood pressure, temperature
  • Personal statistics: Age, weight, BMI

Benefits of EHRs

  • Improved patient care: Quick access to complete records
  • Better coordination: Seamless information sharing
  • Enhanced security: Controlled access to records
  • Reduced errors: Legible, standardized documentation
  • Cost savings: Less paperwork and duplication
  • Improved diagnosis: Complete medical history
  • Patient engagement: Access to own health data
  • Data analytics: Population health insights

Security & Privacy

Our system implements industry-standard security measures to protect sensitive health information:

  • Email Verification: Confirm identity before account activation
  • Password Encryption: Secure password hashing
  • Access Control: Doctors only see their own patients
  • Session Management: Secure login sessions
  • Password Recovery: Secure reset via email
  • Data Validation: Input sanitization and validation
